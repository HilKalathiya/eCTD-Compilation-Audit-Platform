# Rent frontier models, self-host only the plumbing

As of late October 2026 the best-quality stack for this platform is mostly rented: **Claude Opus 5.5** for scientific drafting and GEPA reflection (it tops Artificial Analysis's index at 58, with **Claude Sonnet 5.5** at 56 as the half-price fallback), **AWS Textract Tables** as the scan reader whose cell coordinates count, paired with **Gemini 3.8 Flash** as the independent vision reader, **Claude Haiku 5.5** for classification at $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens, and a local hybrid retriever (BM25, EmbeddingGemma 2, mxbai-rerank-v2) small enough for a laptop CPU. Open weights are mandatory only in sovereign mode, and there they cost real quality: the best self-hostable, non-Chinese, Apache-licensed drafter, Gemma 4 31B, scores 15 on the same index, so that mode belongs on one L40S-class GPU sold as a priced data-control option, not as a saving. Self-hosting is not cheaper at either scale. A student pays tens of dollars a month for APIs and free tiers against $416–588 for an always-on L4; in production, a dedicated H100 never breaks even against open-model APIs at AWS or Azure on-demand prices and needs roughly 150–240 Module 3 dossiers a month on specialist clouds, while at ten customers it would sit about 5% used and cost $2.50–4.30 per million tokens, roughly what Sonnet 5.5 costs, for a model scoring 12 instead of 56. The laptop should hold only the validator, parser, embedder and reranker; drafting and vision run through APIs on public data for the student build and, in production, through Bedrock `eu.`/`us.` profiles, in-region Textract and Google Cloud's `eu` multi-region, because Anthropic's own API cannot keep inference in the EU. Quality is less certain than price: no public benchmark measures regulatory drafting or pharma tables, and no October 2026 frontier model has a current document-parsing score, so a 30–50-item in-house eval must make the final call.

## Opus 5.5 leads drafting, and its premium is trivial per dossier

On Artificial Analysis's main leaderboard (26 Oct 2026), **Claude Opus 5.5 scores 58 and Sonnet 5.5 scores 56**, while Claude Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 4 Argon all score 53 ([AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)); GPT-6.1 Sol reportedly sits one point below Argon and Astra ([ComputingForGeeks](https://computingforgeeks.com/gemini-4-argon-released-features-benchmarks/)). That ordering rules out the most expensive tier: Fable 5.1 costs $10/$50 per million tokens against $4/$20 for Opus 5.5 and $2/$10 for Sonnet 5.5 ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)), and Anthropic itself says to "start with Claude Opus 5.5 for most workloads" ([Anthropic models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview)). The strongest rival is Gemini 4 Argon, which Google's own model card puts at **88.8 on LABBench 2 (biology lab research) against 73.1 for Opus 5.5**, and well ahead on 256K–1M-token long-context retrieval ([ComputingForGeeks summary of Google's card](https://computingforgeeks.com/gemini-4-argon-released-features-benchmarks/)). Those are vendor-run numbers, and Argon's introductory price **doubles to $4/$20 on 1 December 2026**, the same as Opus 5.5 ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). It earns a slot in the eval, not the default.

No independent benchmark measures regulatory or scientific dossier writing. Independent hallucination scores (AA-Omniscience around 42–43 for Argon and Astra) show frontier models still invent facts when recalling knowledge ([AA comparison](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/comparisons/gemini-4-argon-vs-gpt-6-astra)), which is exactly what the fact-placeholder design guards against. Never inventing a number is an instruction-following property, so the eval that matters is your own: 30–50 sections scored by the validator for placeholder violations, invented values and template conformance. One constraint shapes that eval: **Claude API version `2026-10-21` removes `temperature` and `top_p`**, and sending them returns a 400 error ([Anthropic deprecations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations)). Determinism cannot be forced, so each eval item needs several runs, and the validator, not sampling settings, carries correctness.

Cost does not argue against Opus. My estimate, an assumption rather than a measurement, is **0.5–1M tokens per Module 3 section** (retrieval context, page images and 3–5 drafting passes, with only 50–70K output tokens). On that basis a section costs **$3.00–5.40 on Opus 5.5 and $1.50–2.70 on Sonnet 5.5**, roughly $180–324 versus $90–162 for a 60-section Module 3 before caching. Cache hits bill at 0.05x base input on both models ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)), so repeated guidance and template context costs about 95% less. Covering Modules 2, 4 and 5 as well multiplies the volume but not the ratio. Weave and Takeda's AutoIND study, as summarised in the project's own notes and not re-checked against the paper, reported drafting time falling from about 100 hours to under 4 ([arXiv 2509.09738](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09738)); next to that, Opus's extra $100–160 per dossier is noise. Use **Opus 5.5 by default in production**, Sonnet 5.5 for iteration and for section types where the eval shows parity, and keep **GPT-6.1 Sol** wired in as a second vendor so a deprecation or outage never stalls drafting.

GEPA reflection favours the strongest reasoner even more. An optimisation run makes only a few hundred reflection calls, and most of its tokens go to rollouts of the task model being tuned rather than to the reflector. Assuming 300 reflection calls at ~20K tokens in and ~2K out, a run costs about **$36 on Opus 5.5** and $18 on Sonnet 5.5. Because GEPA should run on public or synthetic dossiers, it can use Anthropic's cheapest global first-party endpoint with no residency constraint. Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra ($10/$50) add cost without a measured gain.

## Two scan readers help only if they fail differently

The cross-check works only when the readers make different mistakes. A practitioner analysis of OCR pipelines shows arithmetic reconciliation catching one misread amount but not two that agree: in its synthetic example a vision model read **18.50 as 18.80** and the totals still balanced ([Dubrov OCR guide](https://slavadubrov.com/blog/2026/03/04/ocr-guide/)). Coordinates are a second reason to split the roles. LlamaIndex's ParseBench found general vision models score **below 8% on visual grounding, against 55–80% for layout-based parsers** ([ParseBench](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/parsebench)). The page and cell bounding box must therefore come from a geometry-first OCR engine, while the vision model supplies an independent value for each cell, matched by row and column header rather than by box.

For that first reader, **Textract Tables** fits best. It costs $15 per 1,000 pages, falling to $10 after the first million ([Textract pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/textract/pricing/)), and returns CELL and MERGED_CELL blocks with row and column indexes, spans, geometry and a **per-cell confidence** ([Textract tables docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/how-it-works-tables.html)), which is what lets you route uncertain cells to a human. It runs in Frankfurt, Ireland, London, Paris, Spain and Zurich as well as the US ([Textract endpoints](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/general/latest/gr/textract.html)). Azure Document Intelligence Layout is the alternative: it returns per-cell polygons and page numbers but documents confidence only per word ([Azure Layout docs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/prebuilt/layout?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)), and its ~$10 per 1,000 pages comes from a community answer because the official pricing page did not render ([Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-in/answers/questions/5927427/azure-document-intelligence-pricing)).

Choosing the second reader is harder, because no current document benchmark covers Claude 5.5, GPT-6 or Gemini 3.8/4 ([AnotherWrapper OmniDocBench 1.5](https://anotherwrapper.com/tools/llm-pricing/evals/omnidocbench-1-5)). The best proxy is the previous generation. On the official OmniDocBench, **Gemini 3 Flash scores 89.29 table TEDS against 82.95 for GPT-5.2** ([OmniDocBench](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench)), and human-preference voting on OCR Arena ranked Gemini 3 Flash above GLM-OCR even though GLM-OCR scores higher on that benchmark ([OCR Arena](https://www.ocrarena.ai/leaderboard)). Claude has no row on any of these. That makes **Gemini 3.8 Flash** the evidence-backed default, from a different vendor than both Textract and the Claude drafter. Assuming ~2,500 image tokens in and ~1,200 out per page, it costs about **$6.40 per 1,000 pages today and $12.80 from 1 January 2027**, when its price doubles to $1.50/$7.50 ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). **Haiku 5.5 would cost about $0.85 per 1,000 pages**, so test it head to head on real pages before paying 7–15x more; thinking tokens, high media resolution and retries can multiply all these figures 2–4x. Gemini 4 Argon is not worth its premium for value reading, since Google's own chart benchmark favours it but independent GDP.pdf document reasoning favours GPT-6 Astra, 31% to 22% ([AA comparison](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/comparisons/gemini-4-argon-vs-gpt-6-astra)).

Open specialist models actually lead pure parsing. **PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (Baidu, Apache-2.0, about 1B parameters) tops OmniDocBench table TEDS at 94.76**, ahead of MinerU2.5-Pro (93.42) and GLM-OCR (92.83) ([OmniDocBench](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench); [PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 card](https://huggingface.co/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR-VL-1.6)). Treat those scores carefully. TEDS rewards overall table structure and text similarity, so a table can score above 90 and still carry one wrong specification digit. Old scans defeat every system, which all score just **29–50 on olmOCR-Bench's old-scans category** ([olmOCR](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr)), so scanned batch records and old stability reports need the most human review. Licences also rule tools out: Datalab's Marker, Surya and Chandra weights carry $2M–$5M revenue or funding caps and a ban on competing with Datalab's API ([Marker licence](https://fossies.org/linux/marker/MODEL_LICENSE); [Chandra](https://github.com/datalab-to/chandra)), which excludes them from a commercial product without a paid licence.

Avoid pairs that share ancestry. GLM-OCR uses PaddlePaddle's PP-DocLayout-V3 detector ([NYU Shanghai RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/glm-ocr-z-ais-0-9b-model-takes-the-top-spot-on-document-understanding-benchmarks/)), so PaddleOCR-VL plus GLM-OCR is weak, as are two Gemini models or any two Datalab tools. Compare normalised typed values (decimals, units, "<LOQ" and "ND" tokens), then add the pharma checks a two-reader comparison cannot provide: the value lies within its stated specification, recomputed means, %RSD and total impurities match the replicates, and batch numbers agree across the CoA, batch record and stability tables. Re-read flagged cells at higher resolution or with a third engine before a human sees them, and calibrate confidences before auto-accepting anything; in the same guide's example, a 0.72 threshold let through a wrong page scored at 0.80 ([Dubrov OCR guide](https://slavadubrov.com/blog/2026/03/04/ocr-guide/)).

## Haiku-class models and a laptop-sized retriever are good enough

Classification and routing is where "cheap is good enough" holds most clearly. **Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna both list at $0.10 input / $0.50 output** per million tokens ($0.05/$0.25 in batch), and Anthropic positions Haiku 5.5 for "classification, extraction, and routing" ([Anthropic models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview); [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)). That is **ten times cheaper than Haiku 4.5** ($1/$5) on the same Anthropic page, and ten million input tokens plus one million output a month costs about $1.50. Two traps apply. Haiku 5.5 bills at 5x once a prompt exceeds 100K tokens, with cache reads counting toward that length ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)), and Bedrock offers no batch mode for Opus, Sonnet or Haiku 5.5 ([Bedrock Haiku 5.5 card](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-card-anthropic-claude-haiku-5-5.html)), so EU classification on Bedrock pays the standard rate plus the 10% regional premium. GPT-6 Luna on OpenAI's EU endpoint does support batch ([OpenAI your data](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)), but batch falls outside zero-retention terms, as the placement section explains. Self-hosting cannot compete here: DeepInfra serves Llama 3.1 8B at $0.02/$0.04 ([DeepInfra](https://deepinfra.com/pricing)), while a self-hosted 8B model on an A10 or A40 costs $0.10–0.30 per million output tokens even at full load. The price collapse also removes the rationale for the project overview's plan to run DeepSeek-R1 locally for "preliminary data parsing".

Guidance retrieval should run locally, because the models are tiny and the ICH and FDA corpus is public. Regulatory text is full of exact identifiers like "Q3D" and "3.2.P.5.1", so hybrid search (BM25 plus a dense model plus a reranker) beats dense search alone. For the dense model, **EmbeddingGemma 2** best fits a stack free of Chinese-origin or non-commercial licences: Google released it on 6 Oct 2026 with 740M parameters, an Apache 2.0 licence, an 8K-token context and multimodal input, though it has no published MTEB score yet ([Google EmbeddingGemma docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/embeddinggemma?authuser=7); [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/google-ships-embeddinggemma-2-740m-multimodal-embedder-apache-20)). Version 1 (308M parameters) fits under 200 MB quantized as a smaller fallback. For reranking, use **mxbai-rerank-v2** (mixedbread, Germany, Apache 2.0, 0.5B or 1.5B) ([Mixpeek rerankers](https://www.mixpeek.com/curated-lists/best-rerankers)). Qwen3-Embedding-8B and Qwen3-Reranker score higher under Apache 2.0 ([Superlinked](https://superlinked.com/blog/qwen3-embedding-reranker-guide)), but they are Alibaba models that some pharma security reviews will flag, and NV-Embed-v2 and jina-embeddings-v3 carry non-commercial licences ([Presenc AI](https://presenc.ai/research/best-open-weight-embedding-models-2026)).

API embeddings would cost almost nothing, $0.005–0.01 per million tokens ([DeepInfra](https://deepinfra.com/pricing)), so the argument for running them locally is not price. Local means the same model runs in cloud and sovereign modes and no customer query leaves the region, which makes it the right replacement for the overview's `text-embedding-3-large`. MTEB averages say little about regulatory search, so label 50–100 of your own queries before trusting any ranking. For the answer step, use Sonnet 5.5 in production; in the student build the Gemini free tier is acceptable, provided only public guidance text and a generic question are sent.

## Sovereign mode fits on one GPU but drafts far worse

A fully open, non-Chinese, commercially licensed stack exists. **Gemma 4 31B** handles drafting and the vision read, **Gemma 4 26B-A4B** (3.8B active parameters) handles fast classification, EmbeddingGemma 2 and mxbai cover retrieval, and Docling (MIT) is the first reader, all under Apache or MIT licences ([Gemma 4 report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02770); [Docling](https://github.com/docling-project/docling)). Gemma 4 31B needs about 35 GB at FP8 (my calculation), so it fits a 48 GB L40S; its 4-bit QAT build is 19 GB and needs about 22 GB at a 32K-token context, which just fits a 24 GB L4 ([Jacar.es](https://jacar.es/en/gemma-4-locally-with-ollama/)). An AWS g6e.xlarge with one L40S costs **$1.861/hr, about $1,359 a month** if left on in US East ([DoiT g6e.xlarge](https://www.doit.com/compute/compute/aws/us-east-1/g6e.xlarge)), against $1.09/hr, about $796 a month, on RunPod ([RunPod](https://www.runpod.io/gpu-cloud/pricing)).

The price of that control is quality. Gemma 4 31B scores **15** and 26B-A4B scores **17** on Artificial Analysis, against **58 for Opus 5.5** ([AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)), and on human-preference text ranking in June, Gemma 4 31B sat **57 Elo behind first place** ([Gemma 4 report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02770)). Artificial Analysis's current index compresses scores, so compare only figures from one snapshot, but the gap is still large. Placeholders narrow it, because numbers never come from the model, yet section structure, justifications and scientific judgement will need more reviewer editing, and customers choosing sovereign mode should be told so.

Stronger open drafters exist, each with a catch:

| Model | AA score | Catch |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.8 27B | 34, more than double Gemma's | Chinese origin; licence unverified; runs on a single GPU |
| Mistral Large 4 | 38 (API preview) | Open weights promised for end of October with no stated licence; 1T total parameters needs a multi-GPU node ([Simon Willison](https://feeds.simonwillison.net/tags/mistral/)) |
| Meta Muse Spark Open | 39 | Needs ~700 GB+ of GPU memory; custom licence requires a separate agreement for AI services above $20M revenue ([Trending Topics](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/muse-spark-open-meta-open-weight-license/)) |

Qwen3.8 27B is the strong pick wherever a customer's security review accepts Chinese-origin weights. The best open model overall, Xiaomi's 1T-parameter MiMo-V2.6-Pro, scores 46 ([AA open-weights](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/open-source)). DeepSeek-R1, the overview's planned local fallback, is superseded by DeepSeek's own V4.1 line (45 on the same page) and carries the same origin flag.

For vision in sovereign mode, pair Docling's geometry with Gemma 4 31B, whose OmniDocBench 1.5 edit distance is 0.131 against 0.365 for Gemma 3 27B ([Gemma 4 report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02770)), although its table accuracy against the specialist parsers is unmeasured. If origin is acceptable, use PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 instead. Mistral OCR 4 offers enterprise self-hosting as a single container with block-level boxes and word confidence ([Mistral OCR 4](https://mistral.ai/news/ocr-4)), making it the commercially supported alternative. Prompts tuned for Claude will not transfer to Gemma, so run GEPA with Gemma 4 as the task model and Opus 5.5 as the reflector on public dossiers in the cloud, then ship the tuned prompts into the sovereign box; no customer data is needed.

## The laptop holds the plumbing, APIs do the thinking, customer data stays in-region

On the student laptop, keep only the validator, BM25, EmbeddingGemma, the mxbai base reranker (about 1 GB) and a parser. Docling runs on CPU across macOS, Linux and Windows ([Docling](https://github.com/docling-project/docling)), and MinerU's basic tier is a 0.8 GB download needing 2 GB of RAM ([MinerU](https://github.com/opendatalab/MinerU)). A Gemma 4 E2B QAT model (4.3 GB) is enough for offline smoke tests, or E4B (6.1 GB) on a 16 GB machine ([Jacar.es](https://jacar.es/en/gemma-4-locally-with-ollama/)). Skip gpt-oss-20b, which "requires 16 GB" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-oss/)), and olmOCR, which needs a 12 GB+ GPU and a 30 GB Docker image ([olmOCR](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr)). A 7B model on a laptop CPU manages about **3–10 tokens per second** ([dev.to](https://dev.to/ajeetraina/does-ollama-need-a-gpu-f31)), which means 8–30 minutes per 5,000-token draft before prompt processing, so drafting never runs locally.

Because the student build uses real public data, Anthropic's first-party global API is fine, and its 50% batch discount is usable since retention terms don't matter for public data. The Gemini free tier covers 3.8 Flash and the Flash-Lite models, but Google uses free-tier content to improve its products and human reviewers may read it ([Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/terms)); the EEA, UK and Swiss exemption does not apply in India, so send public documents only. Azure for Students gives $100 for 12 months with no card, renewable while studying, and covers Azure OpenAI but not Marketplace offers ([Azure for Students](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-in/free/students/)), which puts GPT-6.1 Sol or Luna within reach for free. AWS promotional credits are less certain: Claude on Bedrock bills through AWS Marketplace ([Bedrock Opus 5 card](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-card-anthropic-claude-opus-5.html)), so credits may not cover it, though that is unverified. GitHub Models was retired on 30 July 2026 ([GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-cloud@latest/billing/concepts/product-billing/github-models)), and a secondary source reports no official Anthropic student credit program ([AIreiter](https://aireiter.com/blog/claude-api-free-credits-every-way)). For OCR, Textract gives 100 table pages a month for three months and Google Document AI the first 1,000 OCR pages free ([Textract pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/textract/pricing/); [Document AI pricing](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/pricing)). For GPUs, Kaggle's roughly 30 free hours a week on T4 or P100 cards ([AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/free-cloud-gpu)) is enough to rehearse PaddleOCR-VL or small Gemma models. Modal's $30 monthly credit buys about 37 L4-hours, and its academic grants are worded for graduate labs, so apply through a professor ([Modal](https://modal.com/pricing)). India's E2E Networks rents an L4 at ₹49/hr plus GST ([E2E](https://www.e2enetworks.com/pricing.md)), which is fine for public-data experiments and never for a US or EU customer's data.

In production, routing is the binding constraint. **Anthropic's API cannot serve the EU**: its `inference_geo` setting offers only `us` (at 1.1x) or `global`, and workspace storage is US-only ([Anthropic data residency](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/data-residency)). EU Claude therefore goes through **Bedrock `eu.` profiles**, which exist for Opus, Sonnet and Haiku 5.5, route only among EU regions, cost 10% more and **have no batch mode** ([Bedrock Sonnet 5.5 card](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-card-anthropic-claude-sonnet-5-5.html); [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). Gemini goes through Google Cloud's `eu` multi-region, which excludes the UK and Switzerland ([Google Cloud data residency](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/resources/data-residency?hl=ja)), and OpenAI's EU endpoint serves Sol and Luna but not Astra at a 10% uplift ([OpenAI your data](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)). AWS GovCloud, which the overview plans around, is US-only and does nothing for EU customers.

Retention terms narrow the options further. None of the paid APIs trains on customer data, and Anthropic deletes API data within 30 days by default ([Anthropic privacy](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-how-long-do-you-store-my-organization-s-data)). Zero retention needs a sales agreement, and **batch is excluded from it at both Anthropic (29-day retention) and OpenAI** ([Anthropic ZDR](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/zero-data-retention); [OpenAI your data](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)). Confidential dossiers therefore run at standard rates, with prompt caching rather than batch as the cost lever. AWS says Bedrock does not store or log prompts and keeps content in-region, but that 2024 statement does not cover cross-region profiles ([AWS blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/alps/security_bedrock)), so confirm it for `eu.` routing.

Pin exact model IDs. Anthropic's dateless IDs from version 4.6 onward are fixed snapshots ([Anthropic model IDs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/model-ids-and-versions)), and the 5.5 models retire "not sooner than" September–October 2027 with at least 60 days' notice ([Anthropic deprecations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations)). OpenAI promises six months' notice for generally available models, while Google's preview models get two weeks ([OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations); [Gemini models](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models)), so keep `gemini-3.1-pro-preview` out of a validated system. Plan to re-validate yearly, and run a scheduled regression suite, because Anthropic warns that changes to its serving infrastructure can shift behaviour even on a pinned ID.

## Self-hosting loses on cost until roughly 150–240 dossiers a month

Rented GPUs are not cheap, and their prices are not falling. A 24 GB L4 costs $0.57–0.83 an hour, and a single H100 costs $2.49–3.99 on specialist clouds but **$6.88 on AWS and $9.08 on Azure West Europe** ([DoiT p5.4xlarge](https://www.doit.com/compute/compute/aws/us-east-1/p5.4xlarge); [AzureSpeed](https://www.azurespeed.com/AzureVmPricing/Standard_NC40ads_H100_v5); [Lambda](https://lambdalabs.com/service/gpu-cloud)); RunPod raised nine price tiers in August 2026 ([PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/runpod/diffs/2026W34)). At full load, one H100 serving gpt-oss-120b through vLLM produces about **8,973 tokens per second**, roughly 23.6B tokens a month ([SemiAnalysis InferenceX](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/run/gptoss-120b-on-h100)). That works out to **$0.12 per million tokens on RunPod and $0.21 on AWS**, against an API price of $0.15/$0.60, about $0.20 blended for an 8K-in, 1K-out call ([Together](https://www.together.ai/pricing); [Fireworks](https://docs.fireworks.ai/serverless/pricing)). An always-on H100 therefore needs this utilisation just to match the API:

| H100 source | $/hr | Utilisation needed to match the API |
|---|---|---|
| Azure West Europe / AWS on-demand | 9.08 / 6.88 | 141% / 106%, so it never breaks even |
| Together dedicated | 5.49 | 85% |
| RunPod SXM | 3.99 | 62% |
| Lambda PCIe | 2.49 | 39% |

At my ~60M tokens per Module 3 dossier, matching the API takes about **240 dossiers a month on RunPod and 150 on Lambda**. Ten customers drafting two dossiers a month use about 1.2B tokens, or 5% of one H100, which puts the self-hosted cost at **$2.47 per million tokens on RunPod and $4.26 on AWS**. That is the decisive comparison: at list prices for the same 8K/1K mix, Sonnet 5.5 costs about **$2.89 per million tokens ($3.18 with the regional uplift)** and Opus 5.5 about $5.78. A lightly used AWS H100 running gpt-oss-120b, which scores 12, costs more per token than renting Sonnet 5.5, which scores 56, in the customer's own region. Reality is worse for self-hosting still. Single-tenant tools run at 4–16 concurrent requests, where measured vLLM throughput is **3–9x below peak** ([TrueFoundry](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/vllm-benchmark)); production needs a second GPU for redundancy plus patching and monitoring; and if the serving stack falls under GAMP 5 computerised-system validation, every vLLM or model upgrade means re-validation.

At student scale the comparison is not close. Drafting ten sections a month on Sonnet 5.5 costs $15–27, a GEPA run tens of dollars and vision on the Gemini free tier nothing, while an L4 left on all month costs $416–588 ([DoiT g6.xlarge](https://www.doit.com/compute/spot/us-east-1/g6.xlarge); [E2E](https://www.e2enetworks.com/pricing.md)).

Self-hosting does win in three narrow places. Embeddings and reranking run on the app server's CPU and cost nothing extra. OCR at high page volume is the second: olmOCR claims under $200 per million pages on a GPU ([olmOCR](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr)) against Textract's $15 per thousand, and by my arithmetic an AWS L4 at ~$588 a month matches Textract at about **39,000 pages a month**, below the 50,000-page ingestion scenario in the project's own overview. The replacement must still deliver per-cell geometry and calibrated confidence, which Docling provides and olmOCR does not. The third is sovereign mode, where the customer pays for the dedicated box as a data-control feature. The one non-cost advantage of self-hosting, weights that never change underneath you, is also available without servers through open models hosted on Bedrock ([AWS Bedrock pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/pricing/)).

## Conclusion

The economics have inverted since the project overview was drafted around Opus 4.6, DeepSeek-R1 on H100s, GovCloud and `text-embedding-3-large`. Frontier small models now cost what open models cost through an API, which leaves only two real reasons to run a GPU: a customer's demand for data control, and OCR page volume. Saving tokens is no longer one of them. What limits the platform now is residency routing and validation effort. Anthropic's API has no EU option, Bedrock's Claude 5.5 models have no batch mode, and batch is outside zero-retention terms everywhere; meanwhile every hosted frontier model in this report reaches its earliest retirement date within about a year.

The durable asset is therefore not any model choice but the evaluation harness: a placeholder-violation eval for drafting, a cell-level agreement test of the two readers on 50–100 real public CoA and stability pages, and a labelled set of guidance queries, all running against a model layer you can swap. With it, moving from Sonnet to Opus, from Gemini to Haiku, or from cloud to sovereign becomes a measured decision rather than a guess. Without it, no benchmark available in October 2026 can tell you whether any of these models is good enough for a regulator.
